Welcome to the Counseling Skills Trailhead

Counseling skills develop through more than reading about what a counselor should do. They grow through observation, practice, reflection, feedback, ethical decision-making, and repeated opportunities to apply what you are learning.

Along this trail, you will practice core skills used in substance use counseling, including screening, assessment, DSM concepts, interviewing, motivational strategies, treatment planning, referrals, documentation, ethics, boundaries, and recovery support.

Each Trail Stop will ask you to think like a helping professional. You may analyze client situations, make counseling choices, identify strengths and concerns, document information, compare responses, and reflect on the impact of your professional decisions.

A Trail Note from Dr. Holdaway

Counseling skills are not developed by memorizing the perfect thing to say. They grow through practice, reflection, feedback, humility, and learning how to remain present when a situation is complex.

Throughout this trail, you will sometimes encounter more than one reasonable response. Your task is to consider the client's needs, available evidence, ethics, professional scope, cultural context, and the possible impact of your choices.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is becoming more thoughtful, more skillful, more ethical, and more prepared to support the people you may someday serve.
