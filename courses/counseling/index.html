Welcome to the Counseling Skills Trailhead

Counseling skills develop through more than reading about what a counselor should do. They grow through observation, practice, reflection, feedback, ethical decision-making, and repeated opportunities to apply what you are learning.

Along this trail, you will practice core skills used in substance use group counseling, including psychoeducation, trauma-informed facilitation, integrative counseling, group development, therapeutic dynamics, relationship building, structured activities, ethical decision-making, professional boundaries, culturally responsive practice, and group leadership.

Each Trail Stop will ask you to think like a helping professional. You may analyze group situations, make facilitation choices, identify strengths and concerns, practice responding to members, compare possible interventions, and reflect on the impact of your professional decisions.

Pack for Counseling Practice

This trail is designed to help you move from knowing group counseling concepts to practicing how those concepts may be used with real people in real group settings.

👂

Listen Carefully

Notice what members say, what they do not say, how they respond to one another, and what the group may need from you.

🧭

Use Professional Judgment

Group counseling rarely has one perfect response. Consider context, evidence, ethics, client needs, and group process.

💬

Facilitate Intentionally

Practice deciding when to teach, ask, reflect, redirect, link members, structure an activity, or allow the group to work.

🌱

Support Growth

Create group environments that respect autonomy, recognize strengths, reduce stigma, encourage connection, and support recovery.

Stages of Group Development Crossing

Follow a group from formation through transition, productive work, and ending. Practice recognizing what members may need at different stages and how facilitator leadership, structure, trust, conflict, cohesion, and member independence may change over time.

Trail Stop 6

Group Dynamics and Relationships Lookout

Observe the group as a living interpersonal system. Identify cohesion, universality, roles, norms, alliances, communication patterns, peer modeling, interpersonal learning, conflict, belonging, and other dynamics that can strengthen or interfere with therapeutic change.

Group Relationships Planning Camp

Practice creating the conditions for healthy group relationships. Consider screening, composition, group agreements, participation, trust, communication, member roles, conflict prevention, cohesion, and facilitator decisions that influence the emotional climate of the group.

Trail Stop 8

Leadership Techniques Practice Range

Practice the tools group counselors use to create participation and movement. Explore rounds, dyads, structured activities, exercises, processing questions, linking members, redirecting discussion, drawing out quieter members, and choosing techniques that match the purpose of the group.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 9

Ethics and Legal Responsibilities Pass

Work through realistic group counseling decisions involving informed consent, confidentiality and its limits, member privacy, documentation, mandated responsibilities, client rights, professional competence, supervision, and ethical decision-making in substance use treatment.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 10

Boundaries and Professionalism Ranger Station

Practice maintaining warm, respectful helping relationships without losing professional boundaries. Consider self-disclosure, dual relationships, social media, favoritism, rescue behavior, gifts, contact outside group, counselor authority, role clarity, and the appropriate use of consultation and supervision.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 11

Managing Challenging Group Behaviors Rapids

Practice responding when group participation becomes difficult. Work with silence, monopolizing, interrupting, conflict, advice-giving, resistance, side conversations, emotional escalation, inconsistent attendance, disruptive behavior, and other situations without shaming or unnecessarily excluding members.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 12

Diversity and Special Populations Vista

Practice culturally humble and responsive group leadership. Consider how identity, age, culture, race and ethnicity, gender, disability, socioeconomic conditions, trauma, co-occurring concerns, language, family responsibilities, spirituality, access, and lived experience may affect group participation and treatment.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 13

Advanced Group Leadership Expedition

Bring together structure, therapeutic factors, leadership style, member needs, group dynamics, activities, processing, feedback, cultural responsiveness, ethics, and clinical judgment. Practice deciding when to lead, when to redirect, when to invite the group to respond, and when to allow the process to unfold.

Opening Later
Trail Stop 14

Group Session Design Field Lab

Design a complete substance use counseling group session from beginning to end. Select a population and purpose, develop measurable objectives, choose appropriate interventions, plan the opening, activity and processing sequence, anticipate member needs, and develop a meaningful closing and evaluation strategy.

A Trail Note from Dr. Holdaway

Group counseling skills are not developed by memorizing the perfect thing to say. They grow through practice, reflection, feedback, humility, and learning how to remain present when a group situation is complex.

Throughout this trail, you will sometimes encounter more than one reasonable facilitator response. Your task is to consider the members' needs, the purpose and stage of the group, available evidence, ethics, professional scope, cultural context, and the possible impact of your choices on the entire group.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is becoming more thoughtful, more skillful, more ethical, and more prepared to create group environments in which people can learn, connect, practice, and grow.
