Foundations of Addiction

Begin with Curiosity

Your First Trail Assignment

Before we study treatment, recovery, prevention, or counseling, we need to examine the foundation underneath all of them: what addiction is, how substances differ, how society responds to substance use, and how our own language can influence the people we hope to help.

This Trail Stop is not about memorizing one definition of addiction. You will gather evidence from several perspectives and then decide what that evidence means for you as a future helping professional.

There are six Field Stations. You may explore them in any order. Your progress and Trail Journal are saved automatically in this browser.

Trail Briefing

This experience introduces psychoactive substances, patterns of substance use, historical and policy influences, stigma and language, addiction science, and recovery.

By the End of This Trail Stop, You Should Be Able To:

  • Identify major categories of psychoactive substances.
  • Distinguish substance use from patterns that warrant additional assessment.
  • Recognize how history and policy shape responses to substance use.
  • Identify stigmatizing language and replace it with person-centered language.
  • Explain why addiction should be understood through multiple biological, psychological, social, and contextual lenses.
  • Connect respectful language and recovery-oriented thinking to helping-professional practice.
