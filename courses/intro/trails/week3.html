Trail Briefing

Brain pathways are networks of interconnected neurons that allow signals to travel between brain regions. Neurotransmitters carry chemical messages from one neuron to another, and psychoactive drugs can interfere with that signaling in different ways.

Repeated drug exposure can also lead the brain to adapt. In reward-related dopamine systems, the brain may reduce receptor availability and increase dopamine transporters, making the system less responsive. With opioids, tolerance also develops through cellular adaptations as the nervous system attempts to restore balance in the continued presence of the drug.

By the End of This Trail Stop, You Should Be Able To:

  • Identify basic parts of a neuron and explain their role in neural communication.
  • Explain how neurotransmitters carry signals across a synapse.
  • Describe selected dopamine, serotonin, GABA, and glutamate pathways and functions.
  • Explain how inhibitory and excitatory neurotransmission help regulate brain activity.
  • Describe how repeated drug exposure can change dopamine signaling and neural connections.
  • Explain tolerance as a physiological adaptation rather than simply “wanting more.”
  • Apply neuroscience concepts to respectful, person-centered addiction counseling.

Case File: Riley

Riley has been using prescription opioids in ways not directed by a medical provider. Riley says the same amount does not seem to have the same effect anymore and reports taking more to get the effect that used to occur with a smaller amount.

Riley says: “Everyone keeps saying I just keep choosing more. Maybe I’m just weak.”
