Trail Briefing

Privacy and confidentiality help build trust and protect clients from stigma, discrimination, and inappropriate exposure of sensitive information. In substance use treatment, federal rules may provide additional protections for records held by programs covered by 42 CFR Part 2.

Use the lessons to practice an ethical response: understand your role, share only when a valid basis allows it, explain limits of confidentiality, and seek supervision when the law or facts are unclear.

By the End of This Week, You Should Be Able To:

  • Identify major legal and ethical responsibilities in substance use treatment settings.
  • Explain why confidentiality is especially important in substance use treatment.
  • Describe common limits to confidentiality, including safety concerns, mandated reporting, and court-related situations.
  • Explain the purpose of 42 CFR Part 2 and why substance use treatment records may receive special privacy protections.
  • Discuss how informed consent, professional boundaries, documentation, and scope of practice support ethical care.
  • Apply basic legal and ethical principles to client scenarios involving confidentiality and treatment decisions.

Required Resource

Review SAMHSA’s overview of statutes, regulations, and guidelines for substance use disorder treatment. Pay particular attention to 42 CFR Part 2 and its rules for protecting and disclosing patient records.

Open the Required SAMHSA Resource HHS: Understanding Part 2

The 2024 Part 2 final rule’s compliance date was February 16, 2026. This activity reflects the updated framework and provides an educational overview; specific duties depend on the applicable law, role, program, and circumstances.

Practice note: This learning activity is not legal advice. State laws, credentials, employer policies, and client circumstances vary. When a real disclosure or reporting question arises, follow current policy and consult the supervisor, privacy officer, or qualified legal professional. In an immediate emergency, follow emergency procedures.
